# 04 — Art direction that stays coherent over 60 scenes

The risk of a long, code-generated video is a *succession of effects with no relation to each other* — a generic equaliser, subtitles on random backgrounds, neon everywhere, slow floating motion. The reference production avoided it with a small number of hard rules. They are reusable; the specific imagery is not.

## The idea comes from the text

The song is about measurement replacing meaning. So the visual world is a **register**: everything alive (data, pulse, breath, spirit, prayer) gets measured, stamped and validated. The one thing that is never measured — a red point at the centre of the screen — stands for the Origin/Axis that the lyrics say never moves.

**Rule 1 — one rule for every object.** *Every object on screen is an instrument of measurement or control*: ruler, caliper, price tag, barcode, dial, histogram, counter, checklist, stamp, progress bar, form, ledger, calculator, search box. No decor that doesn't measure something. When a lyric names something concrete (mountain, ocean, temple, scales), it is *drawn as seen through an instrument*: an altimeter with "????" over a ridge, a sounding line, a temple wearing price tags.

**Rule 2 — one accent colour with a meaning.** Ink `#0c0c0c`, bone `#e8e2d0`, signal red `#e0342b`. Red = *what is currently being measured* (and the key word of each line). Nothing else is red.

**Rule 3 — two worlds, mapped to two voices.**
- **Dark world** (main voice): instruments, hard-edged, condensed uppercase type.
- **Paper world** (echo/choir voice, the parenthesised lines): hand-drawn, wobbling ink lines, handwriting font, stamps that land with an impact. The same subject shown by hand instead of by instrument.
- A **red bar** sweeps the screen when the world changes. Same-world cuts get a small shake instead.

**Rule 4 — the arc is in the dosage.** Start almost empty; instruments accumulate; the climax is a scale that *loses* its weight; in the epilogue everything is stripped away and a hand-drawn flame remains on paper, then black. The video ends by removing what it spent 8 minutes adding.

**Rule 5 — motion has a vocabulary.** Words *slam* (scale 1.55 → 1 in ≈ 0.3 s) on their start time; impacts shake the whole frame; counters tick on beats; the pulse graph plots the song's real energy; hand-drawn lines "boil" 8×/s. Nothing floats lazily without a reason.

**Rule 6 — legibility first.** Text is capped at ≈ 2 lines (size derived from sentence length), always above the objects, with a background-coloured halo. If an object needs the space, the text shrinks, not the object.

## Choosing a style: prototype three

Before building anything long, three 15–25 s prototypes of the *same* lyrics in different directions were rendered:

| Prototype | Idea | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| A — "Register" | brutal typography + measurement instruments | **chosen** |
| B — "Scale" | monumental landscape, parallax, light | rejected |
| C — "Balance" | one symbolic object (scales) carrying a climax | rejected (but its scales idea survived as a scene) |

The human chose A from stills-plus-video and then asked for *more objects, but coherent* — which became Rule 1.

## Things that were tried and corrected

- **Slow floating everywhere** — replaced by cuts, beat-synced motion and several shots per scene (the human: "avoid fixed shots lasting several seconds, but it must stay readable").
- **Decorative chips** (little red rectangles with numbers) — first added to animate long scenes; the human found them "too much, everywhere, some words overflow". They now appear at most every 6.5 s and only in scenes longer than 12 s. See [docs/05-feedback-log.md](05-feedback-log.md).
- **A scene that was "mostly empty rectangle"** (an altar with an unused box) — rebuilt as an altar + dashboard + gauge + chart + falling sticky notes whose target is never reached.
- **A "too schematic" calculator** — redrawn with display, 16 keys that depress, and a receipt printing a running sum.

## Reuse guide

1. Write one sentence: *what is everything on screen, in the world of this song?* (here: instruments of control).
2. Pick two visual worlds that map to two voices or two moods.
3. Pick one accent colour and give it a meaning.
4. List the ~10 reusable objects of your world before drawing a bespoke one.
5. Decide how the video starts (nearly empty) and how it ends (what gets removed).
